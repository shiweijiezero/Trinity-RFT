\subsection{Entropy Instability in Negative Advantage Regimes}
\label{sec:analysis_positive}
Standard GRPO employs variance-based normalization $A_i = (R_i - \bar{R})/\sigma$. In failure-dominated regimes, the instability is not merely a consequence of the quantity of negative samples, but rather the distributional asymmetry of the policy's probability mass combined with gradient conflict.

This phenomenon can be formalized through the lens of optimization direction. Recent theoretical frameworks, such as RED \cite{yao2025group}, establish that in off-policy settings, standard REINFORCE estimators are prone to convergence issues unless the policy update direction is actively steered towards high-reward regions via data weighting. Similarly, empirical findings from DeepSeek-V3.2 \cite{liu2025deepseek32} identify that highly off-policy negative samples can be detrimental, destabilizing optimization by introducing divergence without constructive guidance. While DeepSeek-V3.2 addresses this by masking negative sequences that exhibit high KL divergence, we propose a complementary approach focused on signal dominance.

Consider the critical exploration phase where the model has not yet solidified the correct reasoning path but is confident in erroneous heuristics. In this regime, the sampled group $\mathcal{G}$ is structurally dominated by negative samples ($A_{neg} < 0$) that simultaneously hold high probability mass ($\pi_\theta(y_{neg}) \gg 0$). Conversely, the correct solution $y_{pos}$ lies in the low-probability tail ($\pi_\theta(y_{pos}) \approx 0$) and is rarely sampled. Consequently, the expected gradient update becomes dominated by the destructive term:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathbb{E}[\nabla J] \approx &\underbrace{\pi_\theta(y_{neg}) A_{neg} \nabla \log \pi_\theta(y_{neg})}_{\mathcal{D}: \text{ Dominant Suppression Signal}} \\
&+ \underbrace{\pi_\theta(y_{pos}) A_{pos} \nabla \log \pi_\theta(y_{pos})}_{\mathcal{C}: \text{ Vanishing Positive Signal}}
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Since the positive signal $\mathcal{C}$ is negligible compared to the suppression signal $\mathcal{D}$, the effective update minimizes $\log \pi_\theta(y_{neg})$. This creates a mechanism of blind suppression. Decreasing the probability of the dominant mode $y_{neg}$ ($\Delta \pi(y_{neg}) < 0$) forces the redistribution of probability mass to the rest of the vocabulary $V \setminus \{y_{neg}\}$:
\begin{equation}
\sum_{v \neq y_{neg}} \Delta \pi(v) = - \Delta \pi(y_{neg}) > 0
\end{equation}
Without a strong positive attractor to capture this redistributed mass, it spreads diffusely across the vocabulary, monotonically increasing the policy entropy $H(\pi)$. The model effectively unlearns its structured (albeit incorrect) behavior and degenerates into a high-entropy uniform distribution, leading to the collapse observed in Figure \ref{fig:training_analysis}.

R$^3$L counteracts this asymmetry by enforcing a gradient dominance condition. We introduce the amplification factor $\alpha$ not merely as a heuristic reward booster, but to satisfy the inequality $\alpha \cdot |\mathcal{C}| > |\mathcal{D}|$. By synthesizing a better retry trajectory $y_{retry}$ and explicitly amplifying its weight, we construct a synthetic attractor strong enough to flip the sign of the total expected gradient. This ensures that the probability mass released from suppressing $y_{neg}$ is channeled specifically to $y_{retry}$ rather than scattered, maintaining structural coherence and ensuring the optimization direction remains constructive even in failure-heavy batches.
